**Open a credit.** Accounting → Letters of Credit → Credits. Record the amount,
tolerance, expiry, latest shipment date and — most importantly — the documents
the credit calls for. Apply, then mark it Issued once the bank gives you a
credit number.

**Link it to what it pays for.** Set the credit on the purchase order; bills
raised from that order inherit it. You can also set it directly on a bill.

**Tick documents off as they arrive.** Each one needs its reference — a set
ticked off without document numbers cannot be checked against the presentation
later. While anything required is outstanding, the bill shows a hold and the
payment register refuses to pay it.

There is no override flag, deliberately. The two ways past the hold are to
record the documents, or to detach the credit from the bill. Both leave a trace;
a bypass would not.

**Present and draw.** A utilisation records a presentation. Discrepant is a
normal state, not a failure — list every discrepancy, and accept the drawing if
the applicant waives them. Accepted drawings reduce availability; paid ones
close them out.

**Amend.** An amendment is created as Requested and changes nothing. Accepting
it writes the new expiry, amount or shipment date through to the credit, so the
credit always shows the terms in force and the history shows how it got there.
